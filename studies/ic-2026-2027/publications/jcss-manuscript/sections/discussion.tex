% DS01
The primary interpretation is conditional on the specified null family, response channel, panel design and fixed lag-feature discriminator. For an attained boundary on a complete valid curve, the frozen v1 classification distinguishes a small boundary ($0<a^*\le0.03$) from a large one ($0.03<a^*\le0.90$). A small boundary means that this discriminator separates the declared synthetic laws at a small nominal partner-drift strength; a large boundary means that separation requires a strength farther along the grid. The cut at 0.03 is an internal convention, not a demonstrated effect size. Non-attainment on a complete valid grid at a value of $r$ means only that this discriminator did not reach the target there; stronger inputs, other summaries and other classifiers remain unresolved. Region labels follow the interval-based classification. Interval width is reported separately against the grid-step precision goal and does not by itself change a boundary's region label. The frozen censoring and indeterminacy rules apply to gaps and grid edges. A zero-strength band that excludes 0.5 invalidates the comparison at that value of $r$. A first boundary followed by a point AUC below the target at a higher strength must be distinguished from a sustained boundary. Statements about how the boundary changes across $r$ are descriptive. No part of the curve identifies causal influence in a panel without observed exposure.

% DS02
The comparisons against trait-plus-autoregressive persistence (B) and state-dependent persistence (D) are read side by side only when the main B curve is valid and tier 2 is entirely complete and valid; Results state whether these conditions were met. The pairing matters because, under the independent-partner approximation of Methods, partner drift acts to first order as a nonlinear function of a person's own state that pushes people away from the population centre, more strongly in the tails. To that order, the transported interaction arm is a state-dependent persistence process, the class that D targets with a different functional form. Separation from D can therefore come from that difference in form or from the network dependence that the approximation ignores, and the design does not tell these sources apart. Under the frozen rule, a B lower band strictly above the target at some strength, together with a D upper band strictly below it at every strength of a common gap-free grid, is read as compatible with non-Gaussian persistence and as giving no specific evidence of interaction; a D band spanning the target leaves the comparison inconclusive, and failing to reach the target against D does not establish equivalence. If the global requirements are not met at the close, any reported B boundary retains the main-invalidation mark where applicable, and no comparison is interpreted across mechanisms.

% DS03
The estimand is an oracle quantity: both arms share a known response signal share, so the null cannot adjust the response-channel noise variance. Estimation difficulties have been reported for trait--state--error decompositions \cite{cole2005tso,ludtke2018starts}. Each null is matched to population moments averaged over many simulated panels, rather than to one observed panel. Under normal-theory assumptions, the AUC target of 0.70 corresponds to a single-panel power of only about 0.18 (Table~\ref{tab:auc-translation}). A boundary describes two laws at a known response signal share; it does not imply that an analyst with one panel would detect partner drift at that strength. In the primary comparison, both arms have time-invariant parameters and marginals, although the interaction arm starts without burn-in. They share the response channel, attrition process and category target. Attrition is unrelated to opinion, and masks are coupled only for matching and validation. These assumptions can fail in real panels through attrition related to the item, reliability that varies across waves, changes of mode or wording, period shocks or refreshment samples of different composition. Any of these departures could by itself make two panel laws separable. % DS03 RC17

% DS04
The generator uses one fixed configuration: persistence 0.85, repulsion 0.25, eight random partners, no burn-in and one step per wave. The variants are limited to attraction-only, anchoring and 24-step configurations. Rank transport removes clustering and bipolarization in the population marginal, so the boundary is not transferable to those models as usually understood. The interaction arm's lag profile has not been compared with that of the SHP item. The panels therefore share the survey's geometry but not necessarily its persistence. The SHP also records household co-residence, and household members aged 14 and older complete individual questionnaires \cite{voorpostel2025shp}. A household-coupled generator with a dyadic discriminator remains for future work.

% DS05
The SHP reliability anchor and the simulation's response signal share $r$ are different quantities. The anchor treats the 0--10 responses as continuous, whereas $r$ is defined before rank-based discretization. The anchor model is a latent autoregression without a stable trait; null B includes a stable trait. When a trait is present, simplex-type estimators attribute part of the long-lag plateau to the autoregression. For example, with latent lag correlations $0.5+0.5\cdot0.5^k$ and $r=0.70$, the Heise triplet estimate is about 0.63. The anchor wave is 2004, when the SHP added its first refreshment sample \cite{voorpostel2025shp}, so that wave mixes first interviews with continuing respondents; it is also wave five of the synthetic geometry. The anchor has a model-based Wald interval, rather than a design-based interval. The raw quasi-simplex anchor, the STARTS sensitivity and the synthetic compatibility map must therefore be presented separately. % DS05 RC24
The map depends on the generator, strength and trait--state composition. Its minimum--maximum ranges over ten synthetic replicates are not confidence intervals and do not propagate uncertainty in the empirical anchor. % DS05 RC25
It does not yield an empirically calibrated $a^*(r)$. % DS05 RC26

% DS06
The procedure has separate limits on matching, band coverage and whole-curve validity. In matching, the quarter-standard-error criterion bounds only the empirical residual of equations that meet the root tolerance, except where the floor binds. It is not a population-bias bound. Under their face and validation rules, constrained B, D or E solutions may retain a larger lag-two residual. Centered nuisance variance does not automatically account for this mismatch. For band coverage, the zero-strength gates concern identities. G1b uses zero nuisance variance, and G2 tests dispersion at the declared B/observed cells. Absolute interior coverage and uniform adequacy across families or channels remain unverified. The artificial criterion calibration does not demonstrate a true joint pass probability of at least 0.95. For the whole-curve claim, if the nine zero-strength gates are independent and the procedure is correct, chance alone gives a probability of invalidation of up to about 5\%. The compute close can also invalidate the whole curve. Passing the technical gates does not remove the generator, measurement or exposure limits described here.

% DS07
The protocol was amended after partial calibration exposure, particularly at $r=0.70$. New seeds and sealed gate records constrain further adaptation but cannot make the evaluation fully blind. Results at the other values of $r$ remain part of the prespecified whole curve; they do not remove the exposure of the method to calibration results. In v4, equality of the finite laws at zero strength is an identity of this simulator and its random-number coupling. It does not imply equality of empirical mechanisms or guarantee discrimination at positive strength.

% DS08
Observational equivalence is a substantive limit: different reinforcement and heterogeneous processes can generate the same joint outcome distribution, including grouped observations \cite{gelastopoulos2025}. Even a valid contrast against B therefore cannot distinguish interaction from all non-interaction processes. Comparisons against C, D and E can narrow that interpretation only where their complete grids and zero-strength gates are valid.

% DS09
Response signal shares here define a synthetic response channel. Estimates from other items, modes or error models cannot be assigned to the SHP trust item without item-specific evidence \cite{antoun2026,lucasdonnellan2012}. The positioning search had limited coverage, so this article makes no priority claim. Any tier left incomplete at the fixed compute close limits which comparisons across null families can be discussed, even when the primary B tier completes.
