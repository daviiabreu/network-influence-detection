\subsection{Model verification}\label{sec:verification}

Verification checks whether the implementation follows the frozen protocol; it does not validate the social mechanism against data. Unit tests, procedural gates and a freeze preflight check distinct properties before confirmatory execution. % VER01
The tests cover finite-population identities, transported null laws, profile roots and constrained faces, validation and retry, local nuisance propagation, bands and censoring, fixed scheduling and seed separation. The preflight binds scientific code, protocol, criteria, inventory, seeds and numerical environment. Test identifiers and binding checks are in \hyperref[supp:verification-details]{Supplementary Material: implementation verification record}. % VER02 VER04
Byte-level agreement is claimed only in the bound environment: the recorded Linux check failed frozen-revision numerical tests. This is a material limit on portability (Code and data availability). % VER14
The gates separately check matching, synthetic-score band mechanics, zero-strength behavior, procedure dispersion and local propagation against full refits. The Results section reports the gate outcomes. Later release and reporting wrappers remain outside the frozen closure, and the G1c successor repairs summary serialization only. Actual release time comes from the side receipt, not the frozen record's conventional deadline (\hyperref[supp:execution-records]{Supplementary Material: execution and reporting records}). % VER03 VER15 VER16

Two docking studies compared implementations written from the specification with the frozen project code. Their initial readings were recorded before access to the project source, and their comparison plans were recorded before comparisons; later readings are marked. The phase history was reconstructed from session transcripts, and model timing probes preceded the model study's comparison plan, limiting the strength of a clean-room claim. % VER06
The model study found shared-seed trajectory agreement and law-level equivalence within its declared margins at reduced size. It did not reproduce matching, uncertainty or production sizes, and its equivalence margin is coarser than the matching tolerance. Two numerical ambiguities concerned LDA standardization and heteroscedastic-error normalization. % VER07 VER08
The inference study agreed on core calculations using identical synthetic inputs. However, solver statuses agreed in only 16 of 22 cases; one ambiguity changed acceptance to a gap. Alternative band studentization gave critical values up to 2.1 times larger. A post hoc reconstruction reproduced the project's readings, and two gap-adjacent sustained-boundary decisions differed. The supplementary record retains these discrepancies and the numerical diagnostics. % VER09 VER10 VER11
Matching and feature derivatives on real simulated panels, parts of the control and covariance paths, and statistical coverage remained outside that docking. Agreement between implementations of the same specification cannot detect a shared specification error or shared misreading, and it substitutes for neither the gates nor empirical validation. % VER12 VER13
