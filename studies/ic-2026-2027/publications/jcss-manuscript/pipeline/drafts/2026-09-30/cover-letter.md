## Part I — fixed letter

The Editors
Journal of Computational Social Science

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “Detection boundaries for nominal partner drift against matched persistence in synthetic opinion panels”, as a full-length research paper.

We ask how much nominal partner drift a fixed lag-based discriminator needs to distinguish synthetic opinion-panel trajectories from matched no-network persistence when the response signal share is known to both arms. The question concerns the limits of model discrimination when social exposure is unobserved. Separation under this design is conditional on the declared laws, response channel and discriminator and does not identify causal social influence.

The frozen design uses eleven-wave panels generated by a rank-transported autoregression with bounded-confidence-shaped, standardized drift on an unobserved random network. Its first-order drift depends on individual state. Nulls match short-lag population moments. The primary estimand is the curve of first grid strengths at which the expected panel-level area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), given a validated fit, reaches 0.70 against trait-plus-autoregressive persistence, accompanied by a sustained boundary and simultaneous within-share bands. A hash-bound validation gate must pass before evaluation on reserved seeds.

The methodological contribution is a prespecified framework for assessing detectability in opinion panels under a known response channel, with matched persistence, a fixed discriminator and explicit treatment of invalid units, gaps and censoring. The model description, frozen rules and provenance distinguish what the procedure can resolve from what it cannot establish about social interaction. This addresses the journal's criterion for methodological work applicable to social phenomena through computational analysis; the ODD+D appendix and reproducibility documentation support its replication criterion.

The validation gate passed; 342 of 414 units were complete and valid. Evaluation used new seeds after partial calibration exposure and protocol amendment. Small and large denote interval-based regions divided at 0.03. Against trait-plus-autoregressive persistence, the first boundary was large at all 9 shares; none was small, not attained, censored or indeterminate. The prespecified conditions for comparing separation across persistence models were not met. First and sustained boundaries differed at 2 shares.

The ODD+D appendix describes the model, and supplementary material documents the declared supporting analyses. [AT SUBMISSION: final manuscript length and the supplements actually included, taken from the assembled PDF.]

The simulation panels are synthetic. Data and permitted supporting outputs, together with the code, are archived openly under the MIT licence at [AT SUBMISSION: Zenodo DOI of the deposited version]. The frozen simulation tree and the later reporting modules are identified separately. The exploratory reliability anchor uses Swiss Household Panel data (Living in Switzerland, version 12.0, doi:10.48573/haxy-c230), which readers obtain from FORS through SWISSUbase under its usage contract; only aggregate estimates are shared.

The work described has not been published previously and is not being considered by another journal.

All authors have approved the final manuscript and agree to its submission to the Journal of Computational Social Science.

There are no related manuscripts, prior dissemination or reused material.

No funding was received for this study, and the authors have no competing interests to declare. Ethics approval and consent are not applicable: the study analyses simulated data, its exploratory anchor uses anonymized Swiss Household Panel microdata under the SHP data-use contract, and no data were collected from people.
