\abstract{When exposure is unobserved, opinion panels can show patterns consistent with partner drift or persistence. We map the nominal partner-drift strength $a$ that a fixed lag-based discriminator needs to separate synthetic panels from matched no-network persistence. Eleven-wave panels approximate the Swiss Household Panel's observation pattern. Both processes share a known response signal share $r$, and persistence is matched to drift on short-lag population correlations. The detection boundary $a^*(r)$ is the first grid strength at which the expected panel-level area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), given a validated fit, reaches 0.70 against trait-plus-autoregressive persistence. Small and large denote interval-based regions divided at 0.03.
